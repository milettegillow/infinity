There are two conventions for labelling infinite sets, and they may or may not line up.

The alephs () are every infinite size there is, with each one being the next size up. The beths () are what you get by repeatedly taking power sets.

Every beth is equal to one of the alephs. Nobody can prove which. Saying that is the continuum hypothesis, and it can be neither proved nor disproved.

Large cardinals cannot be reached by any amount of taking power sets and unions of the infinite sets we have already come across.

Whether any of them exist cannot be settled by the axioms of set theory (ZFC). Each one is an extra axiom you either adopt or don't.

The light outside cannot be reached from inside, however long you carry on. An inaccessible cardinal is an infinite size so vast that it cannot be reached by taking unions and power sets of anything that has come before.
